What does the number of modes mean?

A network of N nodes has 3N − 6 internal normal modes (the 6 rigid-body motions are excluded). They are sorted by frequency: the slowest modes are the softest, most collective motions (hinge bending, domain opening and closing); faster modes are increasingly local.

Each conformation is the input plus a random combination of the first M modes. A mode's amplitude is drawn from a Gaussian scaled by 1/√λ (λ = eigenvalue), as in NMSim and ProDy's sampleModes, so the slowest modes dominate. The total amplitude is set by the average RMSD below, not by M: more modes spread the same deformation over more, increasingly local directions.

  • 1–3 modes: the dominant global motions only; use to study one mechanism, or with Traverse to see each mode.
  • 5–20 modes: the usual range for ensembles (e.g. ensemble docking); ligand-induced changes are typically well described by a handful of the slowest modes (Tama & Sanejouand 2001; Bakan & Bahar 2009).
  • 30+ modes: increasingly local, "breathing" fluctuations.

After Analyze modes, the charts show the share of the network's fluctuation captured by the first M modes, how collective each mode is, and, with a second conformation, how much of the real change they describe. See the Documentation tab for details.
